Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia, Ukraine and the United States held a second day of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, their first trilateral meeting since February, with prisoner exchanges and post-war security guarantees on the agenda; President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the first day "constructive", though no agreement was announced (AP, BBC).

World leaders opened the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, where 77 heads of state or government are due to speak; India pressed rich countries to meet climate-finance pledges, and the UN said current plans would cut emissions only about 6% below 2019 levels by 2035 (UNFCCC, AFP).

Anthropic set a $70 to $78 price range for its Nasdaq IPO under the ticker ANT, to raise up to $64.4 billion at a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion; Google and Nvidia will each buy about $10 billion of shares in a $26 billion private placement alongside it (SEC filing, CNBC).

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above forecasts, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said, cementing bets on a Federal Reserve rate rise in December; the S&P 500 fell 0.4% and the Nasdaq 1% (Reuters).

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo passed 11,000, with 5,387 deaths, government data showed, making it the second-largest outbreak on record; the rare Bundibugyo strain has no approved vaccine or treatment (Reuters).

The US Justice Department charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot targeting RAF Fairford in Britain (BBC). In Haiti, Salvadoran troops fired rockets at a compound of the 400 Mawozo gang (Al Jazeera).

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called for a more balanced US-China trade relationship, to de-risk rather than decouple, ahead of a trip to China next week (South China Morning Post).

In Poland, six people were killed and 15 injured in a mass shooting in Krakow, with citizens raising over €1.13 million for injured officers. In South Korea, a bus hit 32 vehicles in Seongnam, injuring 57 people (Yonhap).
