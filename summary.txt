Abu Dhabi prisoner deal, COP31 and a $64.4bn IPO led 11 November.

Russia and Ukraine ended talks in Abu Dhabi agreeing the biggest prisoner release, numbers due Thursday; Jared Kushner said there was 'no trust right now between the sides' (Reuters, AP).

COP31 opened in Antalya; India pressed rich nations on finance, and UN plans would cut emissions ~6% below 2019 levels by 2035 (UNFCCC, AFP).

Anthropic priced 825m shares at $70-$78 in a Nasdaq IPO worth up to $1.4tn; Google and Nvidia buy $10bn each, trading from 19 November (SEC filing, CNBC).

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October and traders fully priced in a December Fed rate rise (BLS, Reuters).

A tanker was struck and Iran said two more hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz; Tehran said the strait stays shut until its seven conditions are met (Reuters).

Marco Rubio told G7 allies the economic campaign against Iran was so devastating there was 'no need for major combat operations' (Reuters, NYT).

DR Congo's Ebola outbreak reached 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths, the second-largest on record; its Bundibugyo strain has no approved vaccine (Reuters).

NATO jets scrambled after drones were detected near Baltic airspace overnight, prompting shelter alerts across the Baltics; one jet fired at a drone but missed (Reuters).

Israel's new parliament was sworn in after a deadlock cost Netanyahu his majority; Gadi Eisenkot pledged to reverse his policies and open a 7 October inquiry (AP, Reuters).

The US charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot at RAF Fairford, after vans were driven there in September (BBC). Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was moved to a military base after Iranian threats to his life (NYT, Reuters). British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia (BBC, AP).

The House voted 211-204 to kill the impeachment of Hegseth; only Republican Thomas Massie backed it (CBS, NPR).

Six were killed, 15 injured in a mass shooting in Krakow (BBC).
