Abu Dhabi peace talks, the COP31 summit and a $64.4bn IPO led the news on Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia, Ukraine and the US held a second day of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, their first trilateral meeting since February; prisoner exchanges and post-war security guarantees were on the agenda, and Zelenskyy called the first day "constructive"; no agreement was announced (AP, BBC).

Leaders opened the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, where 77 are due to speak; India pressed rich nations to meet climate-finance pledges, and the UN said current plans would cut emissions only ~6% below 2019 levels by 2035 (UNFCCC, AFP).

Anthropic set a $70-$78 range for its Nasdaq IPO under the ticker ANT, valuing it at up to $1.4 trillion; Google and Nvidia will each buy $10 billion of shares in a $26 billion private placement (SEC filing, CNBC).

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above forecasts, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said, cementing bets on a Fed rate rise in December (Reuters).

Confirmed Ebola cases in DR Congo passed 11,000, with 5,387 deaths, officials said, making it the second-largest outbreak on record; the rare Bundibugyo strain has no approved vaccine or treatment (Reuters).

The US Justice Department charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot targeting RAF Fairford in Britain (BBC). El Salvador said its forces destroyed the 400 Mawozo gang headquarters in Haiti in a helicopter strike announced by President Nayib Bukele on Tuesday; a reported toll of about 20 is unconfirmed (BBC, Al Jazeera).

The US House voted 211-204 to send an eight-article impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively ending it; only Republican Thomas Massie sided with Democrats (CBS, NPR).

Six people were killed and 15 injured in a mass shooting in Krakow, Poland, where citizens raised over €1.13 million for injured officers. In South Korea, a bus hit 32 vehicles in Seongnam, injuring 57 people (Yonhap).
