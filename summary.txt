Abu Dhabi talks, COP31 and a $64.4bn IPO led 11 November's news.

Russia, Ukraine and the US held a second day of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi; prisoner swaps and security guarantees led the agenda, Zelenskyy called day one "constructive" but no deal was announced (AP, BBC).

The COP31 climate summit opened in Antalya; India pressed rich nations on finance pledges, and the UN said plans would cut emissions ~6% below 2019 levels by 2035 (UNFCCC, AFP).

Anthropic priced 825m shares at $70-$78 in a Nasdaq IPO worth up to $1.4 trillion; Google and Nvidia will each buy $10bn (SEC filing, CNBC).

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October and traders fully priced in a December Fed rate rise (BLS, Reuters).

Oil rose after a tanker was struck off Oman in the Strait of Hormuz and Iran said two more hit mines (Reuters).

DR Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases and 5,387 deaths, the second-largest on record; the Bundibugyo strain has no approved vaccine (Reuters).

Israel's new parliament was sworn in without a government after a deadlocked election cost Netanyahu his majority; ex-army chief Gadi Eisenkot, tasked with forming one, pledged to reverse his policies and open an inquiry into the 7 October attacks (AP, Reuters).

The US Justice Department charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot at RAF Fairford, after vans were driven there in September (BBC). Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was moved to a military base after Iranian threats against his life (NYT, Reuters). British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia; he served at NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps HQ (BBC, AP).

The US House voted 211-204 to kill the impeachment of Defense Secretary Hegseth; only Republican Thomas Massie backed it (CBS, NPR).

Six were killed and 15 injured in a mass shooting in Krakow. Bulgaria mourned an 86-vehicle fog pile-up on the Trakia motorway that killed at least 11 and injured about 50 (BBC, Reuters).
